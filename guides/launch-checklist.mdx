Shipping an app without a launch plan is like opening a restaurant without telling anyone the address. The `app-launch` skill gives you a battle-tested 8-week framework — from ASO groundwork and beta testing through launch day execution and your first post-launch data cycle. This guide walks you through each phase with specific prompts, tasks, and the reasoning behind the timing.

## How to Use the Skill

Start by giving the skill your app context and timeline. It will generate a fully dated checklist tailored to your launch date, budget, and existing audience.

```
/app-launch I'm launching a fitness app in 6 weeks. Create a launch plan.
```

Or with more detail for a richer plan:

```
/app-launch I'm launching a habit tracker for iOS in 8 weeks. I have 200 Twitter followers, no email list, and a $0 paid budget. Target users: productivity-focused adults 25-40 in the US. Create a launch plan.
```

The more context you provide — launch date, budget, pre-existing audience, target countries — the more specific the plan.

## Pre-Launch: Weeks 1–4

### Week 1 — ASO Foundation

The first week is about understanding the competitive landscape before you write a single word of your App Store listing. Don't touch your metadata yet — audit and research first.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Run `/aso-audit` to score your current (or draft) listing
- [ ] Run `/keyword-research` with 3–5 seed keywords describing your app's core function
- [ ] Run `/competitor-analysis` to identify keyword gaps and positioning opportunities
- [ ] Document your primary, secondary, and long-tail keyword tiers

```
Run keyword research for a fitness habit tracker app. Seed keywords: habit tracker, fitness goals, workout log, daily habits, exercise routine. US market.
```

<Tip>
  If you haven't published yet, paste your draft title and description into the audit. The skill will score your draft metadata and flag issues before they go live.
</Tip>

### Week 2 — Metadata, Screenshots, and Icon

Armed with your keyword research, this is the week to actually write and design your listing. Three skills work together here.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Run `/metadata-optimization` with your target keywords — get the three variants and choose one
- [ ] Run `/screenshot-optimization` to get a 10-slot screenshot strategy with design briefs and caption copy
- [ ] Run `/app-icon-optimization` for a design brief and category differentiation guidance
- [ ] Upload everything to App Store Connect (iOS) or Google Play Console (Android)

```
Optimize my metadata for iOS. App: fitness habit tracker. Primary keywords: habit tracker, fitness goals. Secondary: workout log, exercise routine, daily habits. Market: US.
```

<Note>
  Screenshots are the single highest-impact creative element on your listing page. Users decide in under three seconds whether to keep reading. The first screenshot is your billboard.
</Note>

### Week 3 — TestFlight Beta and Feedback Collection

Real users will find problems your team never imagined. A beta round before launch protects your day-one rating and surfaces critical fixes with no public consequences.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Invite 50–200 beta testers via TestFlight (iOS) or Google Play Open Testing
- [ ] Set up crash reporting (Firebase Crashlytics or Sentry)
- [ ] Collect structured feedback — what's confusing, what's broken, what's missing
- [ ] Fix any critical bugs and usability issues
- [ ] Brief beta testers: ask them to be ready to leave an App Store review on launch day

<Tip>
  Beta testers who helped shape the app are your most enthusiastic early reviewers. A launch day ask — "We're live! We'd love your review" — is both authentic and effective.
</Tip>

### Week 4 — Press Outreach Prep and Product Hunt

Press coverage has a long lead time. Journalists receive hundreds of pitches a week — if you reach out on launch day, you've already missed the window for most publications.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Build your press kit: icon (1024×1024), screenshots (all device sizes), description, founder story, key stats
- [ ] Identify 20–30 relevant journalists, bloggers, and YouTubers covering your category
- [ ] Draft personalised pitch emails (run `/press-and-pr` for templates)
- [ ] Draft your **Product Hunt listing**: tagline, description, first comment, media
- [ ] Prepare 10+ social media posts for launch week
- [ ] Write your launch blog post

```
/press-and-pr Help me write a pitch for my fitness habit tracker app launching next week. Target: productivity and wellness journalists at TechCrunch, The Verge, and app review blogs.
```

## Launch Week: Week 5

### 1 Week Before — Final Prep

Submit for review with a buffer. Apple's average review time is 24–48 hours, but complex apps or first-time submissions can take longer. Don't schedule a launch-day announcement before your app is approved.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Submit your app for App Store review — allow 3 business days of buffer
- [ ] Schedule your release: you can set a date in App Store Connect so it goes live automatically after approval
- [ ] Send press pitches with an **embargo date** of your launch day morning
- [ ] Brief beta testers on the launch date and review timing
- [ ] Prepare Apple Search Ads campaign (run `/apple-search-ads`) — ready to activate at launch

<Warning>
  Do not launch on a Friday. App Store editorial teams are smaller on weekends, press is harder to reach, and Product Hunt resets at midnight PST — Friday posts get far less traction than Tuesday–Thursday.
</Warning>

### Launch Day Checklist

Start early. By the time most of your audience wakes up, you want the app live and the announcement engine running.

**Morning:**

- [ ] Confirm the app is live in the App Store / Google Play
- [ ] Send the press embargo-lift email to all journalists
- [ ] Post launch announcement on social media (personal + app accounts)
- [ ] Launch on Product Hunt — post at 12:01 AM PST for maximum voting window
- [ ] Post in relevant Reddit communities, Discord servers, and forums
- [ ] Email your list (even if it's small — these are your most engaged people)
- [ ] Activate your Apple Search Ads campaign

**Throughout the Day:**

- [ ] Monitor App Store Connect for download numbers hourly
- [ ] Respond to every review — publicly and within a few hours
- [ ] Engage with every social media mention and reply
- [ ] Watch crash reports — if there's a spike, be ready to push a hotfix

**Evening:**

- [ ] Thank early adopters publicly — call out Product Hunt supporters by name if possible
- [ ] Share a day-one milestone if you have one worth sharing ("We hit #3 in Health & Fitness!")
- [ ] Plan tomorrow's content

<Note>
  Respond to every review on launch day — positive and negative. A developer who engages with users signals trustworthiness to both future users who read reviews and to Apple's editorial team. Run `/review-management` for response templates using the HEAR framework.
</Note>

## Post-Launch: Weeks 6–8

### Week 6 — Monitor and Respond

The first week of data is noisy. Don't make major metadata changes yet — give your keywords time to stabilise in the rankings.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Check keyword rankings daily — which target keywords are you indexing for?
- [ ] Respond to all new reviews within 24 hours
- [ ] Monitor crash rate and session length in App Store Connect
- [ ] Follow up with press contacts who haven't responded
- [ ] Continue daily social posting and community engagement

```
Check keyword rankings for my app (id: YOUR_APP_ID). Which of my target keywords broke into the top 50? Which am I not indexing for at all?
```

### Week 7 — Competitor Analysis Post-Launch

Now that you're live and have a few hundred (or thousand) installs, you have real standing to benchmark against competitors. This is a different analysis than the pre-launch one — you can now see where you actually sit.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Run `/competitor-analysis` — compare your ranking positions against top 3–5 competitors
- [ ] Identify keyword gaps: terms competitors rank in the top 10 for that you're not indexing
- [ ] Look for creative gaps: screenshot and icon strategies your competitors haven't tried
- [ ] Check if any competitors changed their metadata since launch — they may be responding to you

```
/competitor-analysis Compare my habit tracker app (id: YOUR_APP_ID) against the top 3 competitors in Productivity. Show keyword gaps and any creative opportunities I'm missing.
```

### Week 8 — First Metadata Optimization Update

You now have four weeks of real ranking data. This is when you make your first evidence-based metadata update — replacing underperformers with better-matched keywords identified in Week 7's competitor analysis.

**Tasks:**

- [ ] Identify keywords that climbed to positions 11–30 — these are candidates for promotion to the title or subtitle
- [ ] Remove keywords you're not ranking for at all and have low opportunity scores
- [ ] Run `/metadata-optimization` with your updated keyword strategy
- [ ] Submit the metadata update (iOS title/subtitle/keyword changes require a new app version)
- [ ] Set a recurring calendar reminder for the next keyword review in 4–6 weeks

```
Optimize my metadata for the next update. Here are my current keyword rankings after 4 weeks: [paste your ranking data]. Suggest changes to improve positions 11-30 keywords.
```
