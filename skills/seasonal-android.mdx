Two skills live on this page that work equally well together or independently. **Seasonal ASO** helps you ride calendar-driven search surges — Christmas, New Year's, Valentine's Day, Back to School — by swapping in seasonal keywords and updating your metadata before peak windows hit. **Android ASO** gives you a Google Play-specific optimisation playbook, where the rules are fundamentally different from iOS: the full description is indexed, there's no hidden keyword field, and ratings never reset.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Seasonal ASO">

    ## Capture Seasonal Traffic When It Peaks

    Seasonal keywords can drive 2–3× your normal organic traffic during peak periods — but only if your metadata is updated *before* the surge begins. The seasonal-aso skill gives you a keyword calendar, a timed metadata swap strategy, and the exact promotional-text copy to push without an App Review submission.

    **Invoke:** `/seasonal-aso`

    ### Why Timing Is Everything

    App Store metadata takes 1–3 days to index after submission. Combine that with Apple's review time (typically 24–48 hours), and you need to plan your seasonal updates at least **2–3 weeks before peak**. Update too late and you miss the window; revert too late and your evergreen rankings suffer.

    <Warning>
      Never swap out a keyword that's currently ranking well for seasonal speculation. The skill flags your existing keyword performance before recommending any changes — protecting your evergreen positions is the first rule.
    </Warning>

    ### The Metadata Swap Strategy

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Identify relevant keywords (T-14 days)">
        The skill researches seasonal search volume for terms relevant to your app. It filters by volume spike versus your 30-day baseline, difficulty under 60 (seasonal keywords attract heavy competition), and genuine relevance to your core use case — not every holiday applies to every app.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Plan your metadata changes (T-10 days)">
        Three fields to update — with different risk levels:

        - **Keyword field (100 chars):** Swap out underperforming evergreen keywords for 2–4 seasonal terms. Preserve your best-ranking terms.
        - **Subtitle (30 chars):** Consider a seasonal hook ("Your Holiday Planner") only if your current subtitle is not driving strong rankings.
        - **Promotional text (170 chars):** Always update this for seasonal events — it's instant, requires no App Review, and resets easily.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Brief your creative (T-10 days)">
        Add a seasonal frame or theme to your first 2 screenshots. The skill coordinates with `/screenshot-optimization` for the design brief.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Submit screenshot updates (T-7 days)">
        Screenshots don't require App Review — submit them independently ahead of the keyword update.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Submit keyword/subtitle update (T-5 days)">
        Build in buffer for review time. The 5-day lead ensures your updated metadata is indexed before the peak window opens.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Monitor rankings daily (T-0)">
        Track daily rank movement for your seasonal keywords during the peak window. The skill suggests which metrics to watch.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Revert to evergreen (T+3 metadata, T+5 promo text)">
        Submit your keyword field and subtitle back to evergreen versions within 3 days after peak — these require App Review, so submit promptly. Revert your promotional text within 5 days — it's instant and requires no review. Letting seasonal terms linger past their window can erode your evergreen rankings.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ### Trending Moments (Non-Calendar)

    Beyond the fixed calendar, viral moments — news events, trending topics, cultural surges — create short-lived keyword opportunities. The skill catches these too:

    - Trending windows are typically 3–7 days; you need to act within 24–48 hours
    - **Only update promotional text** for non-calendar trends — it's instant and reversible
    - Never submit a full metadata update for a trend that might fade before it's reviewed and indexed

    ### Seasonal vs. Evergreen Trade-offs

    | Factor | Seasonal | Evergreen |
    |--------|----------|-----------|
    | Search volume | Temporarily very high | Stable year-round |
    | Competition | Very high at peak | Moderate |
    | Risk | Rankings drop after peak | Consistent |
    | Reward | Install spike during window | Sustained organic growth |

    <Tip>
      The best seasonal strategy layers on top of a strong evergreen foundation — it doesn't replace it. Run `/keyword-research` first to establish your core rankings, then use seasonal-aso to amplify during peak periods.
    </Tip>

    ## Optimise for Google Play's Different Rules

    Google Play's search algorithm works differently from the App Store — and the differences are large enough that a separate playbook is essential. The android-aso skill applies Google Play-specific tactics: indexed description strategy, short description optimisation, Play Experiments A/B testing, and rating recovery.

    **Invoke:** `/android-aso`

    ### How Google Play Differs from iOS

    Before applying any tactics, understand the platform's fundamentals:

    | Factor | Google Play | Apple App Store |
    |--------|-------------|----------------|
    | **Keyword indexing** | Title + Short desc + Full desc | Title + Subtitle + Keyword field only |
    | **Hidden keyword field** | None | 100-character field |
    | **Description indexed** | Full 4,000 chars ✓ | Not indexed ✗ |
    | **Ratings** | Continuous — never reset | Reset per version (can request) |
    | **A/B testing** | Play Store Experiments (native) | Product Page Optimisation |
    | **Screenshots** | 2–8 per language | Up to 10 per language |
    | **Feature graphic** | Required (1024×500px) | Not applicable |
    | **Reviews indexed** | Reviews + replies indexed ✓ | Not indexed ✗ |

    <Note>
      There is no hidden keyword field on Google Play. Every keyword you want to rank for must appear naturally in your title, short description, or full description. This makes your copywriting decisions far more consequential than on iOS.
    </Note>

    ### Metadata Optimisation

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Title (30 characters)">
        Lead with your brand name or primary keyword — whichever is stronger in your category. Include one high-volume keyword naturally.

        ```text
        ✅  Headspace: Meditation & Sleep
        ❌  Best Meditation App for You
        ```

        The title carries the highest keyword weight on Play. Don't waste it on a tagline.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Short Description (80 characters)">
        This is the first text users read in search results — before they tap through to your full listing. Pack your two to three most important keywords into a single compelling sentence, and do not repeat the title's primary keyword.

        ```text
        ✅  Guided meditation, sleep sounds & breathing exercises for stress relief
        ❌  The best meditation app — download Headspace today!
        ```
      </Step>
      <Step title="Full Description (4,000 characters — fully indexed)">
        Every word is crawled. Structure it for both the algorithm and human conversion:

        ```text
        [Hook paragraph — 2–3 sentences]
        Core value proposition. Include primary keyword
        in the first 167 chars (shown above the fold).

        [Feature bullets — 5–8 items]
        • [Feature]: [Benefit]
        Use keywords naturally. Vary phrasing — avoid
        exact phrase repetition.

        [Social proof]
        "Trusted by X million users" / awards / press mentions

        [Call to action]
        Download [App Name] today — [value prop].

        [Keyword-rich closing paragraph]
        A natural paragraph using keyword variants,
        synonyms, and long-tail terms.
        ```

        **Keyword density rule:** Your primary keyword should appear 3–5 times across the full description — using the exact match plus variants and synonyms. Stuffing keywords (repetition without context) triggers Google's spam filters and hurts ranking.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ### Feature Graphic (1024×500px)

    The feature graphic is required on Google Play and appears at the top of your listing when no video is present. Treat it like a billboard:

    - Show the single most compelling use case in one image
    - Keep all text large and legible — it renders at small sizes across many surfaces
    - Stay on-brand and consistent with your screenshots
    - Design to work *without* text too, since text may be cropped on some Play Store surfaces

    ### Play Store Experiments (A/B Testing)

    Google Play has native A/B testing built into the Play Console — no third-party tools required. You can test:

    - App icon
    - Feature graphic
    - Screenshots (up to 3 variants)
    - Short description (up to 3 variants)
    - Full description (up to 3 variants)

    <Tip>
      Test one element at a time. Run each experiment for a minimum of 7 days or 1,000 impressions — whichever comes later — before reading results. Access via **Play Console → Store listing experiments**.
    </Tip>

    ### Rating Recovery

    Unlike iOS, Play Store ratings are **permanent** — every rating ever given counts toward your current score, and there's no version-based reset. This makes proactive rating management critical.

    <CardGroup cols={2}>
      <Card title="Respond to Every 1–3 Star Review" icon="message-lines">
        Responding to negative reviews boosts your score algorithmically. More importantly, it invites users to update their review — many do when they see a thoughtful response.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Fix and Announce" icon="wrench">
        When a low-rating issue is fixed, reply to the original review: "Fixed in version X.X — we'd love a second chance." This converts negative reviews into recovery moments.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Time Your Rating Prompt" icon="clock">
        Use the Play In-App Review API (`ReviewManager.requestReviewFlow()`) to request ratings after a clear success moment — not on a cold open or during a frustrating flow.
      </Card>
      <Card title="Localise Your Responses" icon="globe">
        Google indexes developer replies. Reply in the same language as the review to signal quality to both Google and local users.
      </Card>
    </CardGroup>

    ### Localisation on Play

    Google Play indexes each language's description independently — which means each locale is a fresh keyword opportunity. Translate and *localise* (adapt to local search intent and culture), don't just auto-translate. A keyword that works in US English may have higher-volume equivalents in French, German, or Japanese.
