The `/retention-optimization` skill diagnoses where users drop off in your retention curve, benchmarks your metrics against category averages, and produces an actionable plan covering first-session activation, habit formation in days 1–7, engagement deepening in days 7–30, and long-term retention beyond day 30.

## Invoke

```
/retention-optimization
```

## What You Get

- **Retention diagnostic** — current Day 1/7/30 metrics compared to category benchmarks with gap analysis
- **Activation plan** — onboarding flow audit focused on time-to-value and first-session quick wins
- **Push notification sequences** — message-by-message schedule for Days 1, 3, 5, 7, 14, and 30 with copy and trigger logic
- **Churn prevention tactics** — cancellation flow design, win-back campaign templates, and pause-vs-cancel offer strategy

## Retention Framework

| Phase | Days | Core Goal |
|-------|------|-----------|
| **Activation** | 0–1 | Reach the aha moment in under 60 seconds |
| **Habit Formation** | 1–7 | Create a return trigger (notification, streak, content) |
| **Engagement Deepening** | 7–30 | Introduce advanced features progressively |
| **Long-term Retention** | 30+ | Content freshness, community, loyalty signals |

## Industry Benchmarks

| Category | Good D1 | Good D7 | Good D30 |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Games | > 35% | > 15% | > 8% |
| Social | > 40% | > 20% | > 15% |
| Health & Fitness | > 30% | > 12% | > 10% |
| Productivity | > 25% | > 10% | > 8% |
| Finance | > 30% | > 12% | > 10% |

<Note>
  The biggest retention lever for most apps is **reducing time-to-value in the first session**. Users who don't reach the aha moment in session 1 rarely return. Every unnecessary onboarding step, permission request, or account creation screen before the core value is delivered is a retention tax.
</Note>

<Accordion title="How should I structure push notification content?">
  Never send "Come back!" as a standalone message — always pair a return prompt with a specific value reason. Day 1: tip that helps users do something useful. Day 3: personalized data or content waiting for them. Day 7: streak celebration or progress summary. Day 14: feature discovery for something they haven't tried. Day 30: milestone recap. Max 3–5 notifications per week; allow granular opt-out preferences.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What should my cancellation flow look like?">
  When a subscription user tries to cancel: (1) ask why with a multiple-choice prompt, (2) respond to their specific reason — "Too expensive" → offer a discount or downgrade; "Don't use enough" → show usage stats and suggest features; "Missing a feature" → share roadmap and offer to notify on release, (3) offer a **pause** option as the default alternative to cancelling. Forced-retention dark patterns backfire — they increase refund requests and 1-star reviews.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  The **single most predictive signal of long-term retention** is what users do in their first session. Use your analytics stack to identify which first-session action correlates with Day-30 retention — then redesign onboarding to drive every new user toward that action.
</Tip>
