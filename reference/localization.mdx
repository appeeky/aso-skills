The `/localization` skill helps you pick the right markets to enter, conduct fresh keyword research for each locale (not just translations), and produce a culturally adapted metadata and screenshot plan for every target country.

## Invoke

```
/localization
```

## What You Get

- **Market prioritization matrix** — each candidate market scored on size, competition, effort, revenue potential, and strategic fit
- **Per-country keyword research** — locally searched terms (not translated English keywords) with volume and difficulty data
- **Localized metadata drafts** — title, subtitle, and keyword field for each market within character limits
- **Cultural adaptation checklist** — currency formats, imagery guidance, tone adjustments, and right-to-left layout flags

## Example Prompts

```
"Which markets should I localize for first? My app is a habit tracker."
"Run keyword research for Germany and Japan for my meditation app (id: 6447810428)"
"Create a localization plan for the top 5 markets outside the US"
```

## MCP Tools Used

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `get_keyword_suggestions` | Generate locally searched terms for each target market |
| `get_keyword_metrics` | Validate volume and difficulty per country |

## Market Prioritization Tiers

**Tier 1 — Highest ROI for most apps:**
United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, France, South Korea, Brazil, Canada, Australia

**Tier 2 — Strong potential:**
Spain, Italy, Netherlands, Mexico, India, Indonesia, Turkey, Saudi Arabia

<Note>
  The biggest localization mistake is **translating English keywords directly**. Each locale requires independent keyword research. The German word for "budget tracker" is "Haushaltsbuch" (household book) — a direct translation of "budget tracker" has near-zero search volume in Germany. Run the skill per market, not per language.
</Note>

<Accordion title="What does 'each locale gets its own keyword field' mean?">
  Apple's App Store allows a separate 100-character keyword field, title, subtitle, and description for every locale you publish. Each locale is an entirely independent SEO opportunity. Japanese users searching in Japanese, German users searching in German — your keywords must match how locals actually search, not how you'd describe your app in English.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need to localize my screenshots too?">
  Yes, and it matters more than most developers realize. At minimum, translate text overlays into the local language. For higher-priority markets, also adjust currency symbols, date formats, and imagery. Arabic and Hebrew require right-to-left layout adjustments. A localized screenshot set typically lifts conversion 8–15% over the default English version in that market.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  Start with **English-speaking markets** (UK, Canada, Australia) as near-zero-effort wins — you already have localized copy. Then move to German, French, and Japanese, which have strong App Store ARPU and large user bases worth the translation investment.
</Tip>
